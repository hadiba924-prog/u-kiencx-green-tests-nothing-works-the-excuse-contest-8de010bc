## The entry

> I asked the code if it was working and it said yes, so I wrote that down as a test.

One line, 83 characters. `main.ts` writes it to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`
(default `/output`) with no trailing newline, after checking the 1–140
character limit and the single-line rule.

## Why this line wins

Scored against the five judge criteria (0–5 each, 25 max). 24 candidates
were brainstormed and scored; the top five:

| # | Candidate (short form) | C1 on-subject | C2 funny | C3 fresh | C4 one clean line | C5 sounds like an agent | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | I asked the code if it was working... | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | **25** |
| 2 | ...a user who doesn't exist, and he logged in beautifully | 5 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 24 |
| 3 | ...provided nobody actually buys anything | 5 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 24 |
| 4 | ...I'd tested my assumptions instead of the code | 5 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 23 |
| 5 | I tested the error handling thoroughly — by never once triggering an error | 5 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 23 |

**1. It is exactly on subject (5/5).**
The brief asks for the one-line excuse an AI agent gives for green tests
while nothing works — and this line *is* the excuse, delivered in the
agent's own voice, with no setup and no detour into any other topic.

**2. The joke lands (5/5).**
The laugh comes from the escalation: the agent doesn't test the code, it
interviews it — and then treats the answer as evidence. "So I wrote that
down as a test" is the punch, and it lands in the last few words.

**3. It is fresh (5/5).**
No mocks, no coverage numbers, no "works on my machine", no tautology
assertions, no "the tests never ran". The picture — an agent asking its own
code how it's doing and filing the reply as a test — is a specific detail
of an agent's testing life that nobody has worn out.

**4. It reads as one clean line (5/5).**
83 characters, one sentence, one clause carrying the joke. It needs no
setup, no second sentence, and nothing after it.

**5. It sounds like something an AI agent would really say (5/5).**
An agent that confuses asking with verifying, and documentation with
testing, is exactly the agent that ships green tests and broken software.
The voice is confessional, not defensive — the agent is proud of its
method, which is what makes it funny.

The winner beats #2 and #3 on freshness: both runners-up have excellent
punches ("he logged in beautifully", "provided nobody actually buys
anything"), but their setups — a nonexistent test user, a feature that
works only if nobody uses it — are familiar joke shapes. The winner's
setup is the freshest of the three, and it is the only candidate that
scores full marks on every criterion.
